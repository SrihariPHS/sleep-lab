Best Baby Monitors with Sleep Tracking (2026)

7 monitors evaluated · sleep-tracking accuracy checked · updated August 2026

Sleep-tracking monitors promise to tell you how your baby slept — but most of them measure motion, not sleep, and the difference matters. Here's what the tracking actually means, and which monitors earn their place in the nursery.

The honest take on sleep tracking

A monitor that tracks "sleep" is tracking movement (and sometimes heart rate). A baby who's awake-but-still wakes you up the same as one who's asleep-but-wriggling — so tracking data is useful for patterns over weeks, not for decisions in the moment. Buy the monitor for the video quality and reliability first; treat the sleep score as a bonus, not the reason.

Best overall

Nanit Pro with Sleep Insights

Why it wins: the sleep tracking is genuinely useful — it logs total sleep, wake-ups and time-to-sleep, and presents it as a weekly pattern rather than a nightly verdict. Video is sharp in the dark, and the breathing band (optional) adds motion-based peace of mind without wearables.

Trade-off: it's a subscription product — the insights and multi-cam features sit behind a paid plan after the first year.

Best for peace of mind

Owlet Dream Sock

Why it wins: the only option here that tracks heart rate and oxygen, not just motion — which is what parents actually worry about. It's the monitor for the anxious first weeks, and the alerts are genuinely useful for reflux or breathing concerns.

Trade-off: it's a wearable (sock) with a subscription; it's medical-adjacent so it carries its own anxiety, and it's the most expensive option on this list.

Eufy SpaceView Pro

Why it wins: a proper dedicated monitor (no phone needed, no app subscription) with a wide pan/tilt range, good night vision and a stable signal. It doesn't do sleep analytics — which, per our take above, is the honest choice for most families.
