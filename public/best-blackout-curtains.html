Best Blackout Curtains for Nurseries (2026)

Panels, portable blinds & films evaluated · updated August 2026

Daylight is the single biggest enemy of the early-morning wake-up — melatonin drops when light hits the eyes, and a 5am sunrise reads as "morning" to a baby. But most "blackout" curtains leak at the edges and the rod. Here's what actually darkens a room.

Why "blackout" fails, and the fix

Almost every blackout curtain blocks light through the fabric — the problem is the gap: light spills over the top of the rod and around the edges. The fix is always the same: a curtain that covers 15–20cm past the window frame on every side, hung as high as the ceiling allows, and preferably with a valance or pelmet blocking the top gap. A $40 curtain hung right beats a $200 curtain hung tight to the frame.

Frequently asked questions

Do blackout curtains actually work?

The fabric usually blocks light fine — the failure is at the edges. Light spills over the rod and around the sides. Hang curtains 15–20cm past the frame on every side, mounted high, and add a valance or pelmet for the top gap.

How do I stop light leaking around curtains?

Mount wide and high (15–20cm overlap each side, as high as the ceiling allows), use a valance or pelmet for the top, and check the sides at noon with the 3-second test: if you can see your hand, there's a leak.

Are blackout curtains safe for nurseries?

Yes — choose curtains without dangling cords (or secure them out of reach), which is the cord-safety rule that matters. Thermal blackout panels also keep the room cooler in summer.
