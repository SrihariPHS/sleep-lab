What the research actually says

White noise has replicated support in infant sleep research: studies show it helps some babies fall asleep faster and stay asleep through environmental noise (the classic Spencer et al. 1990 finding has been reproduced with modern machines). It is not a cure for sleep problems — it's a tool that removes one variable.

The three things that matter in a machine: volume ceiling (you need to be able to set it loud enough to matter but never past safe limits), sound quality (harsh digital hiss can irritate rather than soothe), and safety (no small parts, cool-running, away from the cot per SIDS guidelines).

Volume & safety rules

Infant sleep guidance is consistent on this: keep the machine at least 200 cm (roughly 2 metres) from the cot, and never run it at maximum volume for more than a few minutes. A useful rule of thumb — if you can hear it clearly from outside the closed nursery door, it's too loud. Machines with fixed volume settings (like the Dohm's dial) make this easier than app-based ones where a tired parent can tap "max".

Safety note: no sound machine replaces safe sleep practice — always place baby on the back, on a firm flat surface, with no loose bedding. White noise is an aid, not a fix for an unsafe sleep environment.

Frequently asked questions

Is white noise safe for babies?

White noise has replicated support in infant sleep research when used sensibly. The rules: place the machine at least 2 metres from the cot, never run it at maximum volume for long periods, and if you can hear it clearly from outside the closed nursery door, it's too loud.

How far should a white noise machine be from the cot?

At least 200 cm (roughly 2 metres). Distance matters more than the machine — it protects baby's hearing while still masking environmental noise.

Which white noise machine is best for babies?

In our evaluation the Yogasleep Dohm Classic is best overall (real fan sound, no digital hiss), the Hatch Rest Go is best for travel, and the LectroFan EVO is the best budget pick.

Can white noise harm a baby's hearing?

Only if used unsafely — max volume, close to the cot, for long stretches. Follow the 2-metre rule and keep volume moderate and white noise is a useful sleep aid, not a risk.
