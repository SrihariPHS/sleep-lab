Baby Sleep Schedules by Age (0–12 Months)

wake windows · nap counts · typical totals · updated August 2026

There is no one-size-fits-all baby schedule — anyone selling you one is selling you anxiety. What exists is a set of well-established ranges: how long babies of a given age can comfortably stay awake, how many naps they typically take, and how much sleep they need in total. Here they are, with the honest caveats attached.

How to read these ranges

Every number below is an approximate, commonly cited range from paediatric sleep guidance — not a prescription. The American Academy of Pediatrics publishes typical sleep-duration ranges (roughly 14–17 hours per 24 for newborns, easing to about 12–16 hours for infants aged 4–12 months), and wake-window charts from sleep consultants cluster tightly around the same figures. Your baby will live somewhere inside these bands, not on a line.

Use wake windows as a guide, not a timer. The real signal is sleepy cues — rubbing eyes, zoning out, tugging ears. A baby who fights a nap at the "right" window may simply need a shorter one; a baby who's content and growing is almost never "behind schedule."

The nap count shifts that catch parents out

Three transitions matter more than the day-to-day schedule. Around 3–4 months, sleep starts to organise into day/night cycles and the famous "four-month regression" hits — it's a developmental milestone, not a failure, and it passes. Around 6–8 months, most babies drop from three naps to two, and the third-nap fight is the classic early sign. Around 12–18 months (just beyond this guide), two naps become one.

Each drop makes bedtime easier in the short term and harder for a week while the extra wake time settles. If naps are suddenly a battle, try stretching the first wake window by 15 minutes before you conclude anything is wrong.

A sample rhythm, not a timetable

To make the ranges concrete, here's a typical 6-month-old's day — illustrative only, not a prescription: wake 6:30am, first nap 9:00 (90 minutes), second nap 12:30 (2 hours), third nap 4:30 (30 minutes, and the first one to go), bed around 7:00pm. The shape matters more than the times: longest awake stretch before bed, shortest nap late in the day, and no nap starting after 5pm unless you enjoy a 9pm party. If your baby's rhythm looks different but the totals land inside the ranges above, that's not a problem — it's a different shape.

What a schedule won't fix

Teething, illness, reflux, separation anxiety and developmental leaps all wreck the most beautiful schedule — that's normal, and adjusting for a few days isn't "regressing." The environment matters more than the clock: a dark room, an appropriate TOG, and a consistent wind-down make every window easier to hit.

The honest bottom line: ranges are ranges. If your baby sleeps at the low end of the total but wakes happy, feeds well and grows, the schedule is fine. Chase the charts and you'll chase your tail — chase the cues and the charts take care of themselves.
